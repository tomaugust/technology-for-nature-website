Technology for Nature Team Website Project Brief

Mission
-------
The mission of the Technology for Nature Team website is to showcase the people, research, outputs, news, and impact of UKCEH’s Technology for Nature Team in a clear, accessible, and maintainable way. It should connect staff, projects, publications, reports, datasets, software, hardware, methods, and news through shared tags and relationships so visitors can explore the team’s work from multiple perspectives.

Objectives
----------
1. Present Team Identity
   - Communicate who the Technology for Nature Team is.
   - Highlight roles, expertise, contact information, link to external profiles, link to social media, year joined, year left (if relevant)

2. Showcase Work and Outputs
   - Describe current and past projects.
   - Share papers, reports, talks, datasets, software, hardware, methods, public engagement, and other outputs in a curated way.

3. Share News
   - Publish updates, announcements, events, awards, project milestones, and new outputs.
   - Keep the homepage fresh with recent activity from the team.

4. Create Cross-Linked Context
   - Enable visitors to traverse relationships: team member → project → output → news item.
   - Use tags and shared metadata to connect items.

5. Keep Content Easy to Maintain
   - Use structured content formats that are easy to update.
   - Support adding images, galleries, summaries, and links with minimum effort.

6. Deliver a Static Website
   - Build a GitHub Pages-friendly static site.
   - Use a lightweight static site approach that can be updated via Git.

Content Overview
----------------
Main Content Types:
- Team Members
  - Name, role, photo, bio, expertise, contact, URL to social media, link to external profile page.
  - Related projects, outputs, and news.
  - Optional hero image and gallery.

- Projects
  - Title, summary, objectives, timeline, status.
  - Description, team leads, collaborators.
  - Related team members, outputs, and news.
  - Gallery of images or visuals.

- Outputs
  - Title, authors, date, abstract/summary.
  - Output type (paper, report, preprint, talk, dataset, software, hardware, method, workflow, public engagement, other).
  - Download link, repository link, or external URL.
  - Related team members, projects, and news.
  - Optional cover image, gallery images, or screenshots.

- News
  - Title, date, summary, body.
  - News type (announcement, event, award, project milestone, new output, media, other).
  - Related team members, projects, and outputs.
  - Optional image.

Supporting Content:
- Homepage
  - Hero section for Technology for Nature Team.
  - Quick links to Team, Projects, Outputs, and News.
  - Latest updates and featured items.

- About
  - Team mission, vision, and UKCEH context.
  - Summary of approach and impact.

- News
  - Announcements, new outputs, events, awards.

Defined Content Categories and Page Types
-----------------------------------------
Primary content categories:
- People
  - Current and former Technology for Nature Team members.
  - Used for staff profiles, expertise discovery, and links to projects, outputs, news, and external profiles.

- Projects
  - Current and past team projects.
  - Used to explain the team's research activity, collaborators, timelines, related outputs, and news.

- Outputs
  - Publications, reports, talks, public engagement outputs, datasets, software, hardware, methods, workflows, code repositories, and other shareable evidence or reusable resources.
  - Used to group formal academic outputs, practical communications, and reusable resources without forcing everything into separate sections.

- News
  - Announcements, new outputs, events, awards, media, and project milestones.
  - Used to keep the site current and provide a lightweight route into recent team activity.

Supporting content categories:
- Themes / Tags
  - Cross-cutting topics such as biodiversity, remote sensing, AI, sensors, data science, monitoring, and decision support.
  - Used for browsing and connecting related people, projects, outputs, and news.

- Static Information Pages
  - Home, About, and any future evergreen explanatory pages.
  - Used for orientation, team context, and UKCEH links.

Page types:
- Homepage
  - Introduces the Technology for Nature Team, highlights featured content, and provides clear routes into the main categories.

- Listing Pages
  - Team, Projects, Outputs, and News.
  - Provide filterable or scannable summaries with links to individual detail pages.

- Detail Pages
  - Individual people, project, output, and news pages.
  - Carry full descriptions, metadata, images, related links, and cross-references.

- Theme / Tag Pages
  - Automatically collect related people, projects, outputs, and news for a theme or tag.

- Search / Browse Page
  - Provides a single discovery route across categories, tags, and relationships.

- About Page
  - Explains the team mission, UKCEH context, approach, and impact.

Site Structure
--------------
Navigation:
- Home
- About
- Team
- Projects
- Outputs
- News
- Search / Tags

Content Model:
- Each content item is an independent page or record.
- Each item includes:
  - Title
  - Short summary
  - Full description
  - Metadata fields
  - Links to related items
  - Tags
  - Images / gallery

Tagging & Relationships:
- Use shared tags and references such as:
  - People
  - Themes (e.g., biodiversity, remote sensing, data science)
  - Project types
  - Output types
  - News types
- Relationship examples:
  - Team member page lists associated projects, outputs, and news.
  - Project page lists team members, outputs, and news.
  - Output page links to authors/creators, related projects, and related news.
  - News page links to related people, projects, and outputs.

Content Management Approach:
- Use structured data files for ease of update:
  - Markdown with frontmatter
  - YAML / JSON / TOML data files
- Maintain a consistent schema for each content type.
- Images stored in a clear /assets/ or /images/ folder.
- Use plain text files so updates can be managed through Git.

Recommended Technical Approach
------------------------------
Static Site Platform:
- Use GitHub Pages for hosting.
- Choose a static site generator that supports content and metadata:
  - e.g. Jekyll, Hugo, Eleventy, or a simple HTML/CSS + data approach.
- Prefer a solution with:
  - markdown content support
  - data-driven listing pages
  - tag filtering
  - easy image handling

Content Schema Example:
- /content/team/member-name.md
- /content/projects/project-name.md
- /content/outputs/output-name.md
- /content/news/news-item-name.md

Each file contains:
- Title
- Slug
- Summary
- Tags
- Related items
- Image references

Technical Architecture
----------------------
Static site generator:
- Jekyll, hosted with GitHub Pages.
- Use Jekyll collections for primary content types:
  - _people
  - _projects
  - _outputs
  - _news
- Use YAML data files where shared controlled lists are useful:
  - tags / themes
  - output types
  - news types
  - project statuses
  - navigation

URL structure:
- /team/ for people listing and /team/[slug]/ for person pages.
- /projects/ for project listing and /projects/[slug]/ for project pages.
- /outputs/ for all outputs and /outputs/[slug]/ for output pages.
- /news/ for news listing and /news/[slug]/ for news pages.
- /tags/[tag]/ or equivalent for tag/theme browse pages.

Relationship rules:
- People can link to related projects, outputs, and news.
- Projects can link to related people, outputs, and news.
- Outputs can link to related people, projects, and news.
- News can link to related people, projects, and outputs.
- Tags/themes should provide cross-category browsing.

Page Template Requirements
--------------------------
Homepage:
- Introduce the Technology for Nature Team.
- Provide clear routes into Team, Projects, Outputs, and News.
- Feature selected projects and outputs.
- Show recent news.
- Include a concise UKCEH affiliation signal.

About page:
- Explain the team mission, UKCEH context, approach, and impact.
- Link into Team, Projects, Outputs, and News.
- Include general UKCEH links where appropriate.
- Do not act as a standalone contact page.

Listing pages:
- Team listing should support scanning by name, role, expertise, and current/former status.
- Projects listing should support filtering by status and theme/tag.
- Outputs listing should support filtering by output_type, theme/tag, date, and status where relevant.
- News listing should support filtering by news_type, theme/tag, and date.
- Listing cards should show title, summary, key metadata, thumbnail where available, and links to related content.

Detail pages:
- People pages should show role, bio, expertise, contact details, external links, and related projects, outputs, and news.
- Project pages should show summary, description, objectives, timeline, status, team leads, collaborators, images, related people, outputs, and news.
- Output pages should adapt metadata labels to output_type, showing download, repository, external links, license, status, tech stack, related people, projects, and news where relevant.
- News pages should show date, news_type, body, related people, projects, and outputs.

Theme / tag pages:
- Show all people, projects, outputs, and news associated with a tag.
- Group results by content type.
- Provide links back to the main listing pages.

Search / browse page:
- Include in the first build as a browse page if full search is not yet implemented.
- Provide grouped access to tags/themes, output types, news types, and project statuses.
- Defer full-text search unless it can be implemented without adding significant maintenance burden.

First-Build Scope
-----------------
Include in the first build:
- Primary navigation: Home, About, Team, Projects, Outputs, News.
- Secondary browse route: Search / Browse or Themes / Tags.
- Jekyll collections: _people, _projects, _outputs, _news.
- Listing and detail templates for all four collections.
- Filters for Projects by status and tag.
- Filters for Outputs by output_type, tag, and date.
- Filters for News by news_type, tag, and date.
- Related-content sections on all detail pages.
- Basic image support for profile photos, featured images, covers, screenshots, and galleries.

Defer unless easy during implementation:
- Advanced full-text search.
- Complex faceted filtering with multiple simultaneous filters.
- Automated external publication imports.
- Dynamic visualisations or JavaScript-heavy interactions.

Design Principles:
- Clean, professional layout aligned with UKCEH style.
- Strong visuals: hero image, team photos, project graphics.
- Responsive design for desktop and mobile.
- Simple navigation and clear call-to-action links.
- Use clear, scannable layouts with restrained metadata blocks.
- Avoid decorative complexity that makes content harder to maintain.
- Use consistent image ratios for listing cards.
- Keep page templates flexible enough for outputs with very different formats, from papers to hardware resources.
- Use UKCEH-aligned colours and typography where practical, while keeping the first build lightweight.

Evaluation Criteria
-------------------
Content Quality:
- All required sections are present: Team, Projects, Outputs, and News.
- Each page has a clear summary, full description, tags, and relations.

Usability:
- Visitors can easily find related content from any page.
- Navigation is intuitive and consistent.
- Tagging enables meaningful browsing across categories.

Maintainability:
- Content updates are simple with markdown/data files.
- New team members, projects, outputs, and news items can be added without code changes.
- Image additions are straightforward.

Technical Delivery:
- Website builds successfully as a static GitHub Pages site.
- Content loads correctly and pages are accessible.
- Images and galleries work properly.

Launch Readiness:
- Deployment is configured and tested.
- Documentation exists for future updates.
- Site is ready for UKCEH public visibility.

Notes
-----
- Use the team name exactly as Technology for Nature Team.
- Emphasize UKCEH affiliation on the About page and footer.
- Make sure team member bios and project summaries are easy to edit.
- Keep the tagging model flexible enough to support people linking to outputs, news, and projects.
